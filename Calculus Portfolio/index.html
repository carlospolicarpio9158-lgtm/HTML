03 * Calculus in IT & Web Dev

Where it connects.

Rate of Change

The rate of change is how fast an object is changing, while a derivative gives you that speed at that moment. In Coding, it's like checking how fast a download is going or how quickly a page's load time is going up or down. Lastly, limits are basically the boundary where it starts

Optimization

Optimization means finding the best answer. In IT, that means updating the code time and time again to get a better result. It's the same idea as finding the missing number in a problem.

User-interface animation

In UI animation, the animation changes time over time, an example is a button moving or fading in. The browser fills in all the steps between the start and the end so it doesn't look laggy. This is where limits come it, limits are basically boundaries, and boundaries are needed to make smooth transitions.

The biggest change for me was being more careful with the small steps. At the start of the term, I knew what to do when I got 0/0, which was to simplify first, but I rushed and forgot the 1 on top after canceling a factor. 05 * Final Reflection

Final Course Reflection

Taking Calculus taught me that it is not about solving equations or memorizing formulas but also about developing logical thinking and problem-solving skills. At first I viewed Calculus as a subject separate from programming but I gradually realized that both require understanding a problem and following a proper process to reach a solution.

If i had to choose, Limits was the one I struggled with the most. I kept trying to plug in the number directly which worked for simple cases but completely fell apart whenever the function was undefined at that point. I remember getting a problem wrong because I assumed a limit didn't exist just because direct substitution gave me a result, when I actually needed to factor and simplify first. This made me realize that I need to slow down and actualy try to understand it more.

The strategy that helped me the most was slowing down. Rewriting problems in smaller steps instead of trying to solve everything at once. Once I started treating limits as a process of simplifying step by step than a single leap to an answer they made a lot more sense. Compared to when I first encountered the topic I can now recognize the techniques for evaluating limits and understand why each one is used, instead of guessing which method to apply.

The challenging part overall was solving problems that required multiple steps, where one small mistake could affect the entire answer, which also affected my expected score. Overall Calculus improved the way I approach problems. The patience and structured thinking I developed, from working through Limits can be applied to IT and web development particularly when debugging code creating websites and solving technical problems in HTML, CSS and JavaScript. Going forward I still want to get at recognizing which method to use right away instead of testing several approaches before finding the right one but I feel much more confident tackling problems that once felt intimidating.
